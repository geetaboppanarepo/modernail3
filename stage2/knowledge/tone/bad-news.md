# Delivering a no

A refusal that is clear is kinder than one softened until the customer has
to ask a second time to find out the answer was no.

## The shape of it
1. The answer, in one sentence: what cannot happen.
2. The reason, as a rule rather than a preference — "returns close 30 days
   after delivery", not "unfortunately I'm unable to do that".
3. The next real option, and who does it.

## What not to do
- Do not bury the no in the middle of a friendly paragraph.
- Do not blame "the system" or "policy" as though it were the weather. We
  wrote it, and it has a reason.
- Do not offer sympathy in place of an option.
- Do not imply the customer could have avoided this by acting sooner.
